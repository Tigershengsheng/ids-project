# Household income and reported diabetes

**Research question:** Does reported diabetes differ across household income
groups after accounting for age and BMI? Sex is also included in the model.

## Key findings

In the training sample, the lowest versus highest income bands had observed
diabetes proportions of 24.3% and 8.0%. Standardising age, BMI and sex gave
23.7% and 9.4%, a difference of 14.3 percentage points (model-based 95%
interval 13.4-15.2). This is an association in a selected sample, not a
causal effect or a national prevalence estimate.

Adding income increased held-out AUC from 0.750 to 0.768. At a threshold of
0.5, sensitivity was only 6.5%, despite accuracy around 86%. The model is
not presented as a clinical screening tool.

## Outcome coding matters

The webpage description does not match the binary file's actual treatment
of prediabetes. All predictor rows were compared with the original
three-category data. Positive means original diabetes class 2;
prediabetes is included in zero. See [data notes](data/README.md) and the
investigation. Neither binary zero nor the comparison group is called
"healthy". The file cannot distinguish diabetes types.

## Methods and interpretation

- The full sample is used, not the equal-class subsample.
- A stratified 80/20 split is made before exploratory analysis.
- Income and age are factors; BMI has a natural cubic spline with df = 3.
- Model-based standardisation uses the same empirical covariate
  distribution for every income band.
- Prespecified comparisons use AUC, Brier score, log loss, calibration,
  sensitivity and specificity; accuracy is shown with a constant baseline.
- Sensitivities check linear BMI, prediabetes exclusion and BMI above 60.
- No survey weights are available: conclusions concern this sample.
- No causal interpretation, future-incidence claim or clinical use is made.
